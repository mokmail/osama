# Reading levels: ladder and conventions

Use this to turn a vague request ("einfach", "for everyone", "like in school") into concrete writing rules. Pick one level, say nothing to the user, and apply it consistently for the whole document.

## Ladder, simplest first

| Level | Typical request wording | Sentence shape | Vocabulary | Terms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy Read / Leichte Sprache | "Leichte Sprache", "Easy Read", "für Menschen mit Lernschwierigkeiten" | One statement per line, one clause, often one sentence per bullet | ~1,000 most common words | Every term explained once, in its own sentence |
| einfache Sprache / Volksschule | "auf dem Niveau von Volksschule", "einfach erklärt", "wie für ein Kind" | 6–12 words, main clause only, one idea each | everyday vocabulary | Every technical term explained at first use |
| Plain language for the general public (B1) | "plain language", "verständlich für alle", "für Laien" | up to ~15 words, one subordinate clause at most | common words, jargon only if explained | key terms explained briefly |
| Simplification for professionals (B2) | "less jargon", "shorter", "executive-readable" | normal | domain vocabulary kept | only obscure terms glossed |

Rule of thumb for Volksschule / einfache Sprache: replace every subordinate clause with its own sentence, and every abstract noun with a verb ("die Durchführung der Messung" → "Wir haben gemessen").

## Conventions for German targets

- Address consistently: "du" only for children's material, otherwise "Sie".
- Avoid Konjunktiv and modal hedging; say what is or what happened.
- Avoid parentheses: split into its own sentence ("(Das ist ein Messverfahren.)" → "Das ist ein Messverfahren.").
- Percentages, units and big numbers go into words or get an everyday scale: "100 %" → "100 Prozent"; "ein Mikrovolt ist ein Millionstel Volt"; "913.501 MWh" → "rund 913.500 Megawattstunden".
- Foreign-language abbreviations (CMC, EPREL, BIPM) are spelled out once with a short German gloss, then the abbreviation may be used.
- Compound nouns get broken up: "Punzierungskontrolle" → "die Kontrolle von Punzen (Stempeln in Schmuck)".
- Legal and official titles stay verbatim ("Bodenschätzungsgesetz 1970", "Delegierte Verordnung (EU) 2023/1669") — they are names, not vocabulary.

## What stays identical at every level

Names of people and organisations, project and programme titles, dates, laws and regulation numbers, measured values and their units, place names, and any figure the reader is meant to act on. Simplification changes the sentence, never the fact.

## Self-check for one paragraph

1. Read it aloud. Any sentence you run out of breath on is too long.
2. Is there a word the target reader could not explain to someone else? Explain it or replace it.
3. Does the paragraph still carry every fact from the original paragraph? If something vanished, it was a summary, not a rewrite.
